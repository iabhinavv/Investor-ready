2. What we collect

Information you give us

  • When you book a call through Calendly: your name, email address and anything you choose to tell us in the booking form, such as your company and stage.
  • When you email, phone or message us on WhatsApp: your name, contact details and what you write.
  • When you email us your Scorecard result: your score and the statements you ticked. The Scorecard itself runs in your browser; we receive nothing unless you choose to send the email.
  • When you become a client: contact and billing details, and the company documents you share with us (for example the cap table, share register, contracts and accounts). These can include personal data about your shareholders, employees and contractors, such as names, addresses, shareholdings and salaries.

Information collected automatically, only with your consent

If you accept advertising or measurement cookies, Google and Meta tags on this site collect your IP address, device and browser type, the pages you visit, the site that referred you, ad-click identifiers (such as Google's gclid and Meta's fbclid), and actions such as clicking "Book a call". The Cookie Policy lists each cookie.

Information collected by our hosting and fonts

Our website host (GitHub Pages) records your IP address in server logs for security. Our fonts are served by Google Fonts, so your browser sends your IP address to Google when a page loads. Neither sets advertising cookies.

We do not intentionally collect sensitive personal data (such as health, religion or biometric data). Please don't send it to us.

4. Advertising: Google Ads and Meta Pixel

We advertise on Google and on Meta (Facebook and Instagram). If, and only if, you accept advertising cookies, this site loads:

  • The Google tag (Google Ads), to measure conversions, such as a call booked after clicking our ad, and to build remarketing audiences so Google can show our ads to people who have visited this site.
  • The Meta Pixel, to measure conversions from Meta ads, and to build custom audiences of site visitors and similar ("lookalike") audiences.

Google and Meta use cookies to show our ads on other websites and apps based on your visit here. They receive the data described in section 2 and use it under their own privacy policies, in some cases for their own purposes. For data collected on this site through the Meta Pixel, Meta and we are joint controllers for the collection and transfer of the data, under Meta's Controller Addendum; Meta is responsible for what happens to it afterwards.

Contact lists and matching. If we use Google's enhanced conversions or Customer Match, or Meta's advanced matching or Custom Audiences, we share contact details (such as your email address) in hashed form so the platform can match them to its users. We do this only where you've given consent or the law otherwise allows it, and never for people who have opted out.

Your choices. Decline or withdraw advertising cookies at any time in Your privacy choices. You can also manage ads in Google My Ad Center and Meta Ad Preferences, and opt out of many companies' interest-based ads at aboutads.info (US) and youronlinechoices.eu (EU and UK). Read how Google uses information from sites that use its services and Meta's Privacy Policy.

We honour the Global Privacy Control (GPC) browser signal: if your browser sends it, advertising cookies stay off unless you turn them on yourself.

Do we "sell" or "share" personal data? We never sell personal data for money. Under some US state laws, letting advertising platforms collect data through cookies for cross-context behavioural advertising can count as "selling" or "sharing". You can opt out at any time through Your privacy choices or with GPC.

5. Who we share it with

  • Service providers that run parts of our business for us: Calendly (scheduling), Google (email and documents, fonts, Google Ads), Meta (WhatsApp messaging, Meta Pixel), GitHub (website hosting) and the data room tool used in your engagement.
  • Licensed partners (lawyers, tax advisers and registered valuers), only with your permission and only what they need for work you've asked for.
  • Professional advisers such as our accountants and lawyers, under a duty of confidentiality.
  • Authorities, when the law requires it or to protect our legal rights.
  • A buyer or successor, if our business is ever sold or reorganised, under the same protections as this policy.

We don't share client documents with investors or anyone else unless you tell us to. You control who sees your data room.

6. International transfers

We are based in Ghana, and several of our providers (including Google, Meta, Calendly and GitHub) store data in the United States and other countries. When data moves across borders, we rely on the safeguards your law recognises. These include the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses and the UK International Data Transfer Addendum, providers certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and its UK extension, and the transfer conditions in the laws of Ghana, Nigeria, India, Singapore and the UAE. Ask us for details of the safeguard for a specific transfer.

We sign an NDA before we review any company documents, limit access to our team and named partners, and share documents through access-controlled tools, not open links. No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your personal data and the law requires it, we'll notify you and the relevant regulator.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to:

  • know what personal data we hold about you and get a copy;
  • have it corrected or completed;
  • have it deleted;
  • restrict or object to how we use it, including objecting to direct marketing at any time;
  • receive it in a portable format;
  • withdraw consent;
  • opt out of targeted advertising and of any "sale" or "sharing";
  • appeal a decision we make about your request; and
  • complain to a data protection regulator.

To use any of these rights, email experimentsav@gmail.com. We'll reply within one month, or within any shorter period your law sets. We may need to verify your identity first. You can use an authorised agent where your law allows. We won't treat you differently for using your rights.

10. Rights by country and region

European Economic Area and United Kingdom. The EU GDPR, or the UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018, apply, along with the ePrivacy rules and the UK's PECR for cookies. You can complain to your local supervisory authority; in the UK, that's the Information Commissioner's Office.

United States. If you live in a state with a comprehensive privacy law (such as California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia or Texas), you have the rights listed in section 9, including the right to opt out of targeted advertising and "sale" or "sharing". In the last 12 months we have collected identifiers (name, email, IP address, online identifiers), commercial information (services you bought), internet activity on this site (with consent), and professional information (your role and company). We collected these from you, your devices and our ad platforms, for the purposes in section 3. We disclose them to the categories of recipients in section 5. We don't collect sensitive personal information for inferring characteristics.

India. Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, you can access a summary of your data, correct or erase it, withdraw consent, nominate someone to act for you, and use our grievance process. Our grievance contact is Abhinav Verma (details in section 14). If we don't resolve your complaint, you can go to the Data Protection Board of India.

Singapore. Under the Personal Data Protection Act 2012, you can access and correct your data and withdraw consent. You can complain to the Personal Data Protection Commission.

United Arab Emirates. Under Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of Personal Data, you have rights of access, correction, erasure, restriction and objection. You can complain to the UAE Data Office.

Ghana. Under the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843), you can access and correct your data and object to processing. You can complain to the Data Protection Commission.

Nigeria. Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act, 2023, you have rights of access, correction, erasure, portability, objection and withdrawal of consent. You can complain to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.

The problem

"Can you share the data room?" shouldn't start a two-week scramble.

The request usually comes right after a good meeting, when the investor's interest is at its peak. Founders who have to say "give us a couple of weeks" lose that momentum, and the investor fills the silence with other deals.

A thrown-together folder is almost as bad: missing contracts, three versions of the cap table, a model that doesn't match the deck. An analyst reads disorder in the data room as disorder in the company.

Seed data room checklist

What goes in a startup data room.

The eight folders we build, and what belongs in each. At pre-seed and seed, keep it lean; Series A adds depth, not new folders.

1. Company

  • Pitch deck and one-page teaser
  • Company overview and org chart
  • Product roadmap

2. Legal and corporate

  • Certificate of incorporation and constitution (articles or bylaws)
  • Board and shareholder resolutions
  • Shareholders' agreement
  • Any group structure chart, if you have a holding company

3. Cap table and financing

  • Current cap table, reconciled to the share register
  • Every SAFE, convertible note and previous round document
  • Option plan and grant records

4. Finance

  • Financial model and assumptions
  • Historic accounts and latest management accounts
  • Bank statements, burn and runway
  • Tax filings and registrations

5. Commercial

  • Key customer and supplier contracts
  • Pipeline and KPI pack: revenue, cohorts, churn
  • Pricing and any partnership agreements

6. Intellectual property

  • IP assignments from every founder, employee and contractor
  • Trademarks, patents and domain ownership
  • Open-source and third-party licence summary

7. Team

  • Founder and key team bios
  • Employment and contractor agreements (templates plus key hires)
  • Hiring plan

8. Compliance

  • Licences and regulatory approvals your sector needs
  • Privacy policy and data protection approach
  • Any disputes or claims, disclosed up front

Staged access. Not every investor needs every file on day one. We set up a first-look view (deck, overview, model summary, cap table summary) for early conversations, and a full diligence view (contracts, IP, detailed financials) that opens only once there's a term sheet or serious intent.

What to leave out

Over-preparing has a cost too. Early-stage investors don't need years of board minutes or legal opinions, and stuffing the room with them slows reviewers down. We keep it to what investors at your stage actually read, and make sure every number in it matches the deck.

The data room is where the rest of the work comes together: the clean cap table, the financial model and the due-diligence pack. That's why we build it last in the Sprint.

FAQ

Questions about the data room.

Which data room software should we use?

For pre-seed to Series A, a tool with view tracking and access control is enough: DocSend, Papermark, Google Drive with careful permissions, or a dedicated virtual data room if you prefer. We build in whichever you choose; the structure matters more than the tool.

When should we create a data room?

Before your first investor meeting. Investors often ask for it after the first or second meeting, and the speed of your answer is a signal in itself.

What if we don't have some of these documents?

That's normal. The gap list shows what's missing, who should provide it and how urgent it is. Anything that needs a lawyer goes to your counsel, or to one of our licensed partners.

Is our data safe with you?

We sign an NDA before we see anything, and only our team and named partners access your documents.

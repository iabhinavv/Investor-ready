The problem

The wrong structure can stall a term sheet you've already won.

A US fund wants to invest, but its documents assume a Delaware corporation and your company is a private limited in Bengaluru, Accra or Lagos. Now the round waits while lawyers in two countries work out a flip under a deadline, with tax and exchange-control rules nobody priced in.

The opposite mistake is just as costly. Founders flip because "everyone does", then find their customers, licences and future IPO plans were all in their home country. Undoing a flip can be far more expensive than doing one, and several large Indian startups have paid heavily to move back.

What a "flip" is

A flip makes a new foreign company the parent of your existing operating company. Founders and investors swap their shares in the local company for shares in the new parent, which then owns the local company as a subsidiary. The business doesn't move; the ownership does.

What it involves

  • Incorporating the new parent and appointing its registered agent
  • A share swap or transfer, valued and approved by the board and shareholders
  • Exchange-control and foreign-investment filings in your home country
  • Tax analysis of the swap, and of how profits and IP move between the companies
  • Moving IP, contracts and employee options to the right entity
  • Rebuilding the cap table at the parent level

India

Flipping an Indian company involves foreign-investment and overseas-investment rules, a valuation of the shares being swapped, and capital-gains tax questions for the founders. Indian companies can also raise from foreign investors directly, and a growing number of startups have moved their holding company back to India ahead of a domestic listing. The right answer depends on your investors, customers and exit plans.

Ghana, Nigeria and wider Africa

African startups raising from international funds often use a Delaware, UK or Mauritius holding company. Local exchange-control, tax and sector licensing rules decide how the operating company sits underneath, and in regulated sectors such as fintech, licences may need to stay with the local entity.

FAQ

Questions about corporate structure.

Do we need a Delaware company to raise from US investors?

Many US venture funds and accelerators strongly prefer one, and some require it. Others invest in UK, Singapore or Cayman structures. Ask your target investors early; the memo maps which structure fits the investors you're actually talking to.

How long does a Delaware flip take?

Typically several weeks to a few months, depending on the home country's filings and how clean the existing cap table is. That's why we recommend deciding before you start the raise.

How much does a flip cost?

It depends on the countries involved, the number of shareholders and the tax position. The memo gives you a cost range from licensed partners before you commit.

Can we raise first and flip later?

Sometimes, but every new investor makes a later flip more complex and more expensive. If a flip is likely, doing it before the round is almost always cheaper.

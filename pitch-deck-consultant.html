The problem

Most decks are designed. Few are written.

Deck agencies make slides look good. But an investor reading your deck alone isn't scoring the design. They're asking three questions: is the problem real, is this team the one to solve it, and could this become big enough to matter to their fund? If the slides don't answer those in order, good design only makes the gaps look nicer.

The other common failure is a deck that disagrees with the data room: revenue on slide 9 that doesn't match the model, or a market size nobody can trace. Investors read that as a founder who doesn't know their own numbers.

Guide

The seed pitch deck structure investors expect.

The order matters as much as the content: each slide answers the question the last one raised.

12FinancialsWhere does this go in 3 years?
13The ask and use of fundsHow much, for what, to reach which milestone?

Narrative first, design second

We start with a working session and write the deck as plain sentences: one headline per slide that states the point, not the topic. "Revenue grew 4× in six months on $0 paid marketing" says more than "Traction". Only when the story reads well with no design at all do we design it.

The read-alone test

Many investors see your deck before they meet you, often forwarded inside their firm without you there to explain it. Every slide has to make sense with no voice-over. We test this by handing the deck to someone who has never heard of your company and asking them to tell the story back.

Bottom-up market sizing

"1% of a $50B market" is the fastest way to lose credibility. We build the market size from customers you can name × what they'd pay, so the number on slide 6 can be traced and defended.

Matched to the model and the data room

Every figure in the deck comes from your financial model and every claim has a document behind it in the data room. When the deck, model and data room agree, diligence goes faster.

The teaser

A one-page summary for cold outreach and warm introductions: problem, solution, traction, team and the ask. It's often the first thing an investor sees, and what decides whether they ask for the full deck.

FAQ

Questions about the pitch deck.

How many slides should a seed pitch deck have?

12–15 in the main deck, with an appendix for detail such as cohort data, unit economics and product screenshots. Shorter is better, as long as each investor question is answered.

Do you just design, or do you write the content too?

We write it. The narrative and every headline come first; design follows. If you already have a strong narrative and only want design, the Audit will tell you, and we'll scope the work accordingly.

Can we edit the deck ourselves afterwards?

Yes. You get editable PowerPoint or Google Slides files, built so you can update numbers and screenshots without breaking the layout.

Will you pitch it for us?

No. You pitch every investor yourself; that's what investors want to see. The Sprint includes two "VC Grill" mock sessions so you've rehearsed the hard questions first.

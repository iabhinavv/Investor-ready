The problem

"What valuation are you raising at?" is the question most founders answer worst.

Ask too high and good investors quietly pass, or the round drags until you cut the price anyway. Ask too low and you give away ownership you'll want back at Series A. Pick a number because a friend's company raised at it, and the first "how did you get there?" ends the conversation.

Early-stage valuation isn't exact: there are rarely profits to discount. But it isn't arbitrary either. Investors use a small set of methods, and a founder who can show the working, and land in a sensible range on more than one of them, negotiates from strength.

Guide

How to value a pre-revenue or early-revenue startup.

The four methods we use, what each is good for, and why we use more than one.

The Scorecard method

Start with the average pre-money valuation of comparable pre-revenue startups in your region and sector. Then adjust it up or down by scoring your company against that average on weighted factors: team, size of opportunity, product, competition, marketing and sales channels, and need for more funding. A strong team in a large market can justify a premium; an unproven product, a discount.

The Berkus method

Assign value, up to a fixed cap each, to five risk-reducing elements: a sound idea, a working prototype, a quality management team, strategic relationships, and product rollout or sales. It's simple and transparent, which makes it useful for pre-revenue companies, as long as the caps are adjusted for your market.

The VC method

Work backwards from the exit. Estimate what the company could be worth in 5–7 years (revenue × a sector multiple), then divide by the return a venture investor needs at your stage, often 10–30 times for early rounds. The result is the most an investor can pay today and still make their fund work. This method shows you how investors think, which is why it's the most useful one in a negotiation.

Market comparables

Recent rounds for similar companies at the same stage, in the same sector and geography. Comparables anchor the other methods in what the market is actually paying right now, and they are the first thing an investor will check your number against.

From valuation to round design

A valuation only matters alongside the round size. We work out how much you need for 18–24 months of runway from your financial model, what that costs in ownership at each point in the range, and which instrument fits: a SAFE or convertible note (which defer the price) or a priced equity round. The dilution flows straight into your cap table scenarios.

When you need a statutory valuation

Some valuations are legal requirements, not negotiating tools: a 409A valuation before granting US stock options, or a registered-valuer report for share issues by an Indian company. Those must come from a licensed valuer. We coordinate them through licensed partners and make sure they line up with the round.

FAQ

Questions about startup valuation.

What is a typical pre-seed or seed valuation?

It varies a lot by country, sector and traction, and it moves with the market. That's exactly why we use current comparables for your sector and geography, rather than a headline average from another market.

Should we raise on a SAFE to avoid setting a valuation?

A SAFE defers the price but not the question: the valuation cap is effectively a price ceiling, and investors will still ask how you got there. We help you set a cap you can defend and model what it means at conversion.

Is your valuation memo a legal or tax valuation?

No. It's a negotiating and planning document. Statutory valuations such as a US 409A or an Indian registered-valuer report come from licensed partners, which we coordinate.

Can you value a company with no revenue at all?

Yes. The Scorecard and Berkus methods are designed for pre-revenue companies, and the VC method works at any stage.

The problem

The cap table is the first file investors open, and the easiest to get wrong.

Most early cap tables live in a spreadsheet someone built the week the company was formed. Since then there have been SAFEs at different caps, an advisor promised 1% over coffee, a co-founder who left, and options granted before the pool was approved. Each change made sense at the time. Together they make a document nobody fully trusts.

Investors need the cap table to price the round: their ownership depends on yours. If the numbers move during diligence, the price moves with them, usually against you.

What we find most often

  • A spreadsheet that doesn't match the official share register
  • Advisor or early-employee equity promised but never documented
  • SAFEs recorded at face value, with no conversion modelled
  • Options granted before the board approved the pool
  • A departed co-founder whose unvested shares were never bought back

Guide

How SAFEs, notes and the option pool change who owns what.

The three things that most often surprise founders at the term sheet.

Post-money and pre-money SAFEs convert differently

A post-money SAFE (the Y Combinator standard since 2018) fixes the investor's ownership up front. A $500,000 SAFE at a $5M post-money cap converts to 10% of the company as it stands just before the priced round, whatever other SAFEs you raise. That certainty is good for the investor; for you it means every additional SAFE dilutes the founders, not the earlier SAFE holders.

A pre-money SAFE or a convertible note converts at a price based on the company's value before the new money. Its holders share dilution with everyone else, so their final percentage depends on how much else you raise. Mixing both kinds, at several caps and discounts, is how cap tables become hard to read.

Discounts, caps and interest

A SAFE or note usually converts at whichever is better for the investor: the valuation cap, or a discount (often 15–25%) to the price new investors pay. Notes also accrue interest, which converts into extra shares. We model each instrument at a range of round prices, so you can see the ownership at $6M, $8M or $10M before you negotiate.

The option pool shuffle

Investors usually ask for an employee option pool, often 10–15% at seed, to be created or topped up before their money comes in. That means the pool dilutes existing shareholders, not the new investor, and lowers your effective pre-money valuation. Sizing the pool to a real hiring plan, rather than a round number, is one of the simplest ways to keep more of the company.

How the cleanup works

  1. Collect: share register, certificates, SAFEs, notes, option grants, board minutes and any side promises.
  2. Reconcile: rebuild the cap table from source documents, not from the old spreadsheet.
  3. Model: convert every instrument at a range of round prices, add the pool and show three rounds of dilution.
  4. Paper: give your lawyer a list of anything that needs a signature, approval or filing. Licensed counsel does the legal work; we coordinate it.

The clean cap table then feeds the valuation memo and the data room, which is why it comes early in the Sprint.

FAQ

Questions about cap table cleanup.

What does "clean cap table" mean to an investor?

That every share, option, SAFE and note on the table is backed by a signed document, matches the official register, and converts in a way the investor can model. No surprises, no promises that aren't on paper.

We use Carta (or Pulley, or a spreadsheet). Does that matter?

No. We work in the tool you use, or hand over a clean spreadsheet. Software stores the cap table; it doesn't check that the data in it is right. That's the part we do.

Can you fix undocumented equity promises?

We identify them and work out what each person was promised and what it would cost to honour it. The actual grant or agreement is drafted by your lawyer; we give them everything they need.

How long does a cleanup take?

7–10 business days for most pre-seed to Series A companies, assuming documents arrive within 2 business days of our request.

Do you work with non-US companies?

Yes. We work with companies in the UK, UAE, Singapore, India, Ghana and Nigeria, including those with an Indian or African operating company under a foreign holding company.

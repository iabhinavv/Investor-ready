The problem

You can't fix what you haven't seen. Investors will see it in week one.

Most founders find their gaps the expensive way: an investor's analyst sends a diligence list, and each item turns into a week of chasing a lawyer, an accountant or an ex-employee for a signature.

An investor readiness assessment flips the order. We run the same checks first, privately, so you decide what to fix and when, before anyone with a cheque is watching. Founders who think they are ready often still have gaps. Founders who know they aren't ready get a plan instead of a vague worry.

How we assess readiness

The six areas we score.

These are the six things early-stage investors check, in roughly the order they check them.

1. Corporate and legal

Is the company properly incorporated where investors can fund it? Are the founders' shares issued and documented, board approvals minuted and statutory filings up to date? Is there anything, such as a pending dispute, that an investor's lawyer will flag?

2. Cap table

Does the cap table match the official share register? Is every SAFE, convertible note, option grant and promised advisor stake recorded? Do you know what ownership looks like after this round converts everything?

3. Financials and model

Are the historic accounts clean and reconciled? Does the forecast connect revenue to real drivers such as customers, price and conversion, rather than a growth percentage? Can you explain burn, runway and use of funds?

4. Valuation and round

Is the valuation you plan to ask for supported by a method an investor recognises? Does the round size give you 18–24 months of runway? Is the instrument (SAFE, note or priced round) right for your stage and market?

5. Pitch materials

Does the deck tell the story in the order investors read it? Can it be understood without you presenting it? Do its numbers match the model exactly?

6. Data room and IP

Could you share a complete data room within 24 hours? Has every founder, employee and contractor signed an IP assignment? Are key customer contracts, licences and policies in one place?

FAQ

Questions about the Audit.

What is an investment readiness assessment?

It's a structured review of a startup against the checks investors run in due diligence: legal, cap table, financials, valuation, pitch materials and the data room. The output is a score and a list of gaps. Ours is done by a person, not a quiz, and comes with a written fix plan.

How is this different from the free Scorecard?

The Scorecard is six questions you answer yourself in two minutes. The Audit is us reading your actual documents, the way an investor's analyst would, and writing up what we find.

What do you need from us?

Access to your incorporation documents, share register and cap table, any SAFEs or notes, the latest accounts, your model and deck if you have them, and key contracts. We send one checklist; missing items are a finding, not a problem.

Is the fee really credited to the Sprint?

Yes, in full, if you start a Sprint within 30 days of receiving your report.
